# The Internalization of the *Daimon* (δαίμων): Interiority and the Emergence of the Self from Homer to Plato

## Abstract
This repository hosts an ongoing philological and philosophical research project investigating the **internalization of the *daimon*** and its constitutive role in the emergence of **interiority and the self** in Ancient Greek thought. Moving beyond ethical or purely religious interpretations, this inquiry traces a fundamental ontological and psychological shift: how an external, uncanny agency in Homer gradually collapses inward to form the architecture of human subjectivity, culminating in Plato’s identification of the highest part of the soul (*nous*) with a personal *daimon* (*Timaeus* 90a).

## Core Thesis & Research Questions
1. **From External Agency to Psychic Interiority:** How does the transition from Homer’s fragmented psychic vocabulary (where *daimon* acts upon the hero from without) to Heraclitus’ fragment B119 (*êthos anthrôpôi daimôn*) establish an inner space of selfhood?
2. **The Epistemology of Inner Experience:** What is the ontological status of Socrates’ *daimonion* (*Apology*, *Phaedrus*, *Theages*) as an internal phenomenon—how does it delineate the boundary between the "I" (*autos*) and an inner, non-discursive presence?
3. **The Daimonic Self in Plato:** How does Plato synthesize archaic possession (*theia mania*) with rational self-identity, turning the *daimon* from an external fate into the transcendent core of the individual self (*Symposium*, *Timaeus*, *Republic* X)?

## Methodology (Pure Philology & Philosophy of Mind)
* **Close Reading & Lexical Mapping:** Exhaustive textual analysis of primary passages in Ancient Greek (Homeric, Ionic, and Attic dialects), tracking the semantic evolution of *daimon*, *psychê*, *nous*, and reflexive pronouns (*heauton*, *autos*) to map the discovery of inwardness.
* **Comparative Doxography (*Quellenforschung*):** Isolating authentic Presocratic conceptions of the *daimon* and the soul from later Neoplatonic layers of interpretation.
* **Attic Retroversion & Conceptual Testing:** Experimental translation of modern philosophical passages on selfhood, first-person authority, and inner speech into Classical Attic Greek to test the exact boundaries of ancient psychological vocabulary.
* **Papyrological & Manuscript Comparison:** Reviewing variant readings and critical apparatuses (Oxford Classical Texts) for key passages concerning the soul and the *daimon*.

## Repository Structure (Research Archive)
* `/lexicon` — Annotated tables of primary Greek passages organized chronologically to trace the shift from external agency to inner selfhood.
* `/commentaries` — Close philological and philosophical essays on key texts (Homer, Heraclitus, Plato’s *Alcibiades I*, *Phaedrus*, and *Timaeus*).
* `/retroversions` — Parallel texts (English/German to Classical Attic Greek) testing the limits of ancient terminology for "selfhood", "subjectivity", and "inner voice".
* `/bibliography` — Curated critical bibliography on the emergence of the self, Greek psychology, and the *daimon* (e.g., Snell, Dodds, Vernant, Gill, Long).
